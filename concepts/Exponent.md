# Exponent

## Definition

Given a [[Real Number]] $a$, called the [[Exponent|Base]], and [[Rational Number]] $n$, called the [[Exponent]], the $n-th$ [[Exponent|Power]] of $a$ as demonstrated by the following [[Expression|Expressions]] [^1]

$$
\begin{align}
a^{0} &= 1 \\
a^{1} &= a \\
a^{2} &= a \times a \\
a^{3} &= a \times a \times a \\
a^{n} &= a \times a \times \dots \times a
\end{align}
$$

## Exponent Laws

### Exponent Addition

Given two expressions with the same [[Exponent|Base]] $a$ and two different [[Exponent|Exponents]] $m, n$, the product can be reinterpreted as an expression with the same [[Exponent|Base]] $a$ but the [[Exponent]] is the sum of the original [[Exponent|Exponents]] [^1]

$$
a^{m} a^{n} = a^{m + n}
$$

### Exponent Subtraction

Given two expressions with the same [[Exponent|Base]] $a$ and two different [[Exponent|Exponents]] $m, n$, the quotient can be reinterpreted as an [[Expression]] with the same [[Exponent|Base]] $a$ but the [[Exponent]] is the difference of the original [[Exponent|Exponents]] [^1]

$$
\frac{a^{m}}{a^{n}} = a^{m - n}
$$

### Recursive Exponent

Given an [[Exponent|Exponential]] $a^{n}$, raising this [[Expression]] by a [[Exponent|Power]] $m$ can be reinterpreted as the same [[Exponent|Base]] $a$ raised to the product of the two original [[Exponent|Exponents]] [^1]

$$
(a^{n})^{m} = a^{mn}
$$

### Exponent Distribution

Given an [[Exponent|Exponential]] $(ab)^n$m, this can be reinterpreted as the product or quotient of two [[Exponent|Exponential]] wherein the [[Exponent]] is distributed to the two components [^1]

$$
\begin{align}
(ab)^{n} &= a^{n} b^{n}  \\
\left( \frac{a}{b} \right)^{n} &= \frac{a^{n}}{b^{n}}
\end{align}
$$

### Negative Exponent

Given a quotient of two [[Real Number|Real Numbers]] $a, b$, raised to a negative [[Exponent]] $-n$, the result can be reinterpreted as the [[Reciprocal]] of the quotient with the same [[Exponent]] (but now positive) [^1]

$$
\left( \frac{a}{b} \right)^{-n} = \left( \frac{b}{a} \right)^{n}
$$
